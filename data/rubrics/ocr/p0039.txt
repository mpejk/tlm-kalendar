330. The privileges of votive Masses I class are as follows:
(a) the Gloria and Creed are said;
(b) all non-privileged commemorations are excluded, as
also the oratio imperata of the local Ordinary;
(c) the prayer of an impeded votive Mass is added under
the same conclusion to the prayer of the Mass of the day, so long
as a day mentioned under nn. 1, 2, 3 and 8 in the table of
precedence does not occur;
(d) if the Mass is sung, the solemn tone is used.
II--The Mass of the Dedication at the Consecration of a church
331. The consecration of a church, although by law it can be
done on any day, is more fittingly performed on a Sunday or
feast. It is forbidden, however, on the vigil and feast of Christmas,
on the feasts of the Epiphany, Ascension and Corpus Christi, on
the days between the II Sunday of Passiontide or Palm Sunday
and Easter Sunday inclusive, on Pentecost Sunday and also
All Souls' Day.
332. The Mass of the Dedication in the actual consecration of
a church or oratory is a part of the complete rite of consecration;
therefore it must be said whenever a church or oratory is
consecrated, even on those days when other votive Masses I class
are forbidden.
333. In the Mass of the Dedication in the actual consecration of
a church is added, under the one conclusion, the prayer of the
Mystery or Saint in whose honour the church or oratory has
been consecrated, and no other commemoration is allowed, not
even a privileged one.
334. Other Masses, which are celebrated in the church or
oratory on the day of consecration when the rite of consecration
is finished, can be of the Dedication, as votive Masses I class.

III-Masses during Eucharistic Congresses
335. On each day of a Eucharistic Congress, whether it be
diocesan, regional, national or international, the principal Mass,
provided it is sung (in cantu), may be that of the most holy
Sacrament of the Eucharist, celebrated as a votive Mass I class.
336. In other public celebrations of these Congresses, the Mass
of the most holy Sacrament of the Eucharist may be celebrated
as a votive Mass II class.
337. Individual priests who are present at a Eucharistic Congress
may say the Mass of the most holy Sacrament of the Eucharist
as a votive Mass III class.
IV-Votive Masses in certain extraordinary celebrations
338. The privileges dealt with in this paragraph refer to Masses:
(a) in a Triduum or Novena which is celebrated in honour
of some Saint or Beatus within a year of the canonization or
beatification;
(b) in certain extraordinary celebrations lasting for three
days or a week, on the occasion, for example, of a secular
commemoration. Celebrations of an extraordinary nature in
honour of a Beatus, however, are excluded.
339. For the arrangement of the celebrations mentioned in the
preceding paragraph a special indult from the Holy See is
necessary.
340. On each day of these celebrations the following are allowed:
(a) one sung Mass (in cantu) of the mystery of the Lord,
of our Lady, of the Saint or Beatus in whose honour the
celebrations are taking place, as a votive Mass I class;
(b) all low Masses, as above, as votive Masses II class.
